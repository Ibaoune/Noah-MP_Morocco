## Figure Captions

**Figure 1. Annual mean hydrological response for state variables and ET (2016–2020)**
*Caption:* Spatial distribution of the annual mean hydrological response to SMAP assimilation over northern Morocco (2016–2020) for surface soil moisture ($\Delta$SSM), root-zone soil moisture ($\Delta$RZSM), and evapotranspiration ($\Delta$ET). The panels display the differences for DA-NoCDF minus Open Loop (OPL) (top row), DA-CDF minus OPL (middle row), and DA-NoCDF minus DA-CDF (bottom row). Fluxes (ET) are expressed in mm/day and state variables in m$^3$/m$^3$. Divergent colorbars are centered on zero, with red indicating negative DA-induced changes (drying/decrease) and blue indicating positive DA-induced changes (wetting/increase).

**Figure 2. Winter (DJF) mean hydrological response for state variables and ET (2016–2020)**
*Caption:* Spatial distribution of the winter (DJF) mean hydrological response to SMAP assimilation (2016–2020) for SSM, RZSM, and ET. The panel structure is identical to Figure 1. Red indicates negative DA-induced changes and blue indicates positive DA-induced changes. During DJF, DA-NoCDF indicates a widespread drying effect across the domain.

**Figure 3. Summer (JJA) mean hydrological response for state variables and ET (2016–2020)**
*Caption:* Spatial distribution of the summer (JJA) mean hydrological response to SMAP assimilation (2016–2020) for SSM, RZSM, and ET. The panel structure is identical to Figure 1. Red indicates negative DA-induced changes and blue indicates positive DA-induced changes. During JJA, DA-NoCDF is associated with a slight wetting effect compared to the OPL, while ET increases markedly.

**Figure 4. Annual mean hydrological response for runoff components (2016–2020)**
*Caption:* Spatial distribution of the annual mean hydrological response to SMAP assimilation (2016–2020) for total runoff, surface runoff, and baseflow. The panels display the differences for DA-NoCDF minus Open Loop (OPL) (top row), DA-CDF minus OPL (middle row), and DA-NoCDF minus DA-CDF (bottom row). All variables are expressed in mm/day. Red indicates negative DA-induced changes and blue indicates positive DA-induced changes. Note: CDF matching strongly attenuates the runoff/baseflow response relative to DA-NoCDF. This baseflow response acts as an internal pre-routing diagnostic and should not be interpreted as observed streamflow.

**Figure 5. Winter (DJF) mean hydrological response for runoff components (2016–2020)**
*Caption:* Spatial distribution of the winter (DJF) mean hydrological response to SMAP assimilation (2016–2020) for runoff components. The panel structure is identical to Figure 4. Red indicates negative DA-induced changes and blue indicates positive DA-induced changes.

**Figure 6. Summer (JJA) mean hydrological response for runoff components (2016–2020)**
*Caption:* Spatial distribution of the summer (JJA) mean hydrological response to SMAP assimilation (2016–2020) for runoff components. The panel structure is identical to Figure 4. Red indicates negative DA-induced changes and blue indicates positive DA-induced changes.

**Figure 7. First-order water-balance diagnostic (2016–2020)**
*Caption:* First-order water-balance diagnostic (Precipitation minus ET minus Total Runoff) for the Open Loop (OPL), DA-NoCDF, and DA-CDF experiments across annual, DJF, and JJA periods. Fluxes are expressed in mm/day. The flux-only balance provides a first-order interpretability check, but does not demonstrate exact water-balance closure because temporal storage changes ($\Delta$S) are not explicitly included. NoCDF remains highly valuable as a diagnostic tool rather than a strictly mass-conservative configuration.
