## 3.4 Hydrological response to CDF and NoCDF assimilation

The propagation of SMAP assimilation increments into the deeper hydrological profile reveals distinct behaviors depending on the scaling strategy employed. The DA-NoCDF experiment generally exposes a strong, diagnostic hydrological response. Assimilation updates significantly alter both the surface soil moisture (SSM) and root-zone soil moisture (RZSM) profiles, which subsequently propagate deeply into the hydrological fluxes, notably evapotranspiration (ET) and runoff. This response exhibits a strong seasonal signature. During DJF, DA-NoCDF reduces SSM, RZSM and ET relative to OPL, while increasing pre-routing total runoff mainly through the baseflow-like component. This indicates that the assimilation-induced reduction in near-surface and root-zone water is redistributed within Noah-MP toward the slow runoff pathway, but this response should be interpreted as an internal pre-routing diagnostic rather than as streamflow improvement. During JJA, DA-NoCDF produces a weak positive SSM response, while RZSM remains slightly lower than OPL on average. ET increases markedly, suggesting a stronger surface-flux response during the dry season. Runoff changes remain small and are again dominated by Qsb rather than Qs. 

The runoff response is strongly dominated by the baseflow-like component (Qsb) in the pre-routing Noah-MP outputs, while the surface runoff (Qs) response is comparatively marginal. It is important to note that this baseflow response acts as a deep slow reservoir buffering the assimilation impacts and should not be interpreted directly as improved streamflow, as no explicit routing or streamflow validation is included in this phase.

In contrast to the dynamic response observed in DA-NoCDF, the DA-CDF experiment demonstrates a highly conservative behavior. CDF matching strongly attenuates runoff and baseflow responses relative to DA-NoCDF, although ET remains non-negligibly affected and season-dependent. This attenuation indicates that while CDF matching preserves the climatological distribution of the model, it reduces the raw model–SMAP mismatch signal and therefore limits the diagnostic sensitivity of the hydrological response, filtering out structural discrepancies between the model background and satellite observations. NoCDF therefore remains highly valuable as a diagnostic tool to track these deviations without strictly enforcing mass conservation.

## 3.5 First-order water-balance interpretability

To assess the hydrological fluxes resulting from SMAP data assimilation, a first-order flux-only water balance (Precipitation minus ET minus Total Runoff) was calculated. The flux-only diagnostic supports a first-order interpretation of how DA-induced changes are redistributed among ET and runoff, but it does not demonstrate water-balance closure because storage changes are not explicitly included.

The residual changes introduced by DA-NoCDF highlight where assimilation dynamically adds or removes unmodeled water from the system. For instance, the significant winter drying induced by DA-NoCDF is physically consistent with external water being removed by the assimilation process, which subsequently decreases ET and runoff. Furthermore, the IMERG precipitation forcing and its seasonality provide critical context for interpreting this hydrological response. While strong alignments between increments and precipitation events are observed, rigorous precipitation-error attribution remains a future extension. Ultimately, the DA-NoCDF experiment produces changes in runoff and ET that are consistent with the unmodeled water additions and removals suggested by SMAP, supporting a diagnostic interpretation of the assimilation increments even when a perfectly closed mass balance is not enforced.
